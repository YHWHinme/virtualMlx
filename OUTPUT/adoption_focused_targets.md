# High-Conversion Targets: Adoption-Focused Animal Facilities

This document focuses on organizations that physically house animals and are highly motivated to increase adoptions.

## U.K. Targets (Day Cycle)
*Focus on independent, volunteer-led, and small-scale rescues. Avoid large networks like RSPCA or Dogs Trust.*

- **South West**: Target small rescues in **Devon, Cornwall, and Somerset**. These rural areas often have many independent, family-run sanctuaries.
- **The Midlands**: Look for independent rescues around **Birmingham, Nottingham, and Leicester**.
- **East Anglia**: Focus on small-scale rescue groups in **Norfolk, Suffolk, and Cambridgeshire**.
- **Local Independent Rescues**: Search for "small dog rescue [City/County]" to find volunteer-run organizations.
- **Private Sanctuaries**: Look for specialized animal sanctuaries that operate on a smaller, non-corporate scale.

## U.S. Targets (Time-Zone Based)

### Day Shift (Target: East Coast / Central)
*Call these in your afternoon (approx. 2 PM – 6 PM GMT) to hit their 9 AM – 1 PM window.*
- **Florida**: Massive volume of private rescues and sanctuaries.
- **Texas**: One of the highest concentrations of independent rescues nationwide.
- **Georgia & Ohio**: High numbers of reporting private organizations.
- **Indiana (Indy)**: A strong regional hub for Midwest rescues.
- **Maine**: High density of shelters per capita.

### Night Shift (Target: West Coast & Mountain)
*Call these in your evening (approx. 5 PM – 10 PM GMT) to hit their 9 AM – 2 PM window.*
- **California**: Huge market for private, high-emotional-investment sanctuaries.
- **Oregon & Washington**: Strong animal welfare communities with many independent rescues.
- **Colorado & Arizona**: High volume of niche and breed-specific physical facilities.

## Primary Target: Small-Scale Private Sanctuaries
These are the highest-value targets. They are typically privately owned or family-run facilities that provide long-term care.

### Species Examples for Narrowing Search:
If you want to target specific types of animals, add these to your search terms:
- **Canines**: "Dog sanctuary", "Husky sanctuary", "Senior dog sanctuary"
- **Felines**: "Cat sanctuary", "Feline rescue center"
- **Equines**: "Horse sanctuary", "Donkey rescue", "Pony sanctuary"
- **Farm/Exotics**: "Pig sanctuary", "Goat rescue", "Bird sanctuary", "Reptile sanctuary"

### Why they are lucrative:
- **Emotional Connection**: They view the animals as family; they are more likely to invest in a website if they believe it will save a specific animal.
- **Capacity Issues**: Unlike large shelters, they have limited space. A website that increases adoptions is a direct solution to their biggest problem.
- **Low Tech-Savvy**: Many are run by people who love animals but struggle with technology, making them prime candidates for website services.

### Sanctuary Insights:
- **Websites**: Most have a basic website, but many are outdated, not mobile-friendly, or only have a "Contact Us" page.
- **Hours**: They are generally **not** 24-hour services for the public. They usually have set visiting hours, but the owners live on-site, meaning they are often available to talk.
- **Website Value**: They care deeply about websites because it's how they attract donors and adopters.

## Secondary Target: Breed-Specific Physical Facilities
Focus only on those that have a physical location to house the animals.

### Catch-All Search Terms:
To avoid searching for every single breed (Golden Retrievers, Greyhounds, etc.), use these broad terms:
- "Breed Specific Rescue"
- "Specialty Animal Rescue"
- "Purebred Rescue Center"

## Avoid:
- **Foster-Based Rescues**: (No physical location, already online-focused).
- **Large Municipal Shelters**: (Too much bureaucracy, usually have government-mandated sites).
- **Volunteer-Only Groups**: (No physical housing, usually just looking for more help).
